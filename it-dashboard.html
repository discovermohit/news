The story in one line: the split is still there — and this week it's the Indian side that's winning. By Wednesday, July 29 the macro had turned hostile: the Iran truce broke, the IRGC fired ballistic missiles at US bases, US-Saudi forces struck sites in Iraq, and Brent jumped 3.4% back toward $88 — reviving the exact supply-shock risk that should hurt an import-dependent India. Instead Mumbai ripped 701 points to 77,467, with Nifty IT up ~2% and Infosys, L&T and TCS leading, as both FIIs (₹755 cr) and DIIs (₹1,664 cr) bought. On the global side the mega-caps stayed heavy — the Nasdaq slipped even as the Dow rose on the oil bid, leaving the Magnificent 7 line soft after last week's chip repricing. The structural divergence is the chart: global investors keep treating asset-light Indian services as a hedge against the AI-hardware trade, while the majors still sit ~46% below their 2024 peak. Underneath it all: 205,000+ tech jobs gone in 2026 with 54% of cuts citing AI, Claude Opus 5 shipping as Anthropic's fourth flagship in under two months, and Gartner's IT-spend forecast held at $6.37tn (+14.2%), data-centre systems up 55.8% — a supply-led build-out betting on demand that hasn't fully arrived.

Cloud, Infra & Chips
AI servers set to triple — the biggest line item nobody has ordered yet — Gartner expects spending on AI-optimised servers to triple over five years and become the largest single subsegment of AI spending, as cloud providers expand capacity in anticipation of GenAI and agentic workloads. That word — anticipation — is the whole risk. It's a supply-led build-out betting that the agent demand Forrester keeps failing to find in enterprises will eventually show up. Silicon · Capex
The $2.59tn vs $487bn scope trap — Gartner's $2.59 trillion counts the entire AI stack; IDC's $487bn counts AI infrastructure hardware only. They are not competing forecasts of the same thing, and one compiled analysis calls mixing them the single most common analytical error in technology budgeting. Worth knowing before someone puts both on the same slide this quarter — a 5x gap in a headline is almost always a definitional gap, not a disagreement. Analyst · Forecasts
The chip trade finally cracks — $1tn gone — the structural threat everyone flagged arrived as a violent repricing: custom silicon from OpenAI, Amazon and Cerebras began shipping, an SK Hynix HBM-slowdown report hit memory names, and more than $1 trillion evaporated across semiconductors — SOX −10%, Kospi −9.4%, Micron −$38bn in a session, Intel −21%. Better-and-cheaper, more-contested compute undercuts the pricing power baked into chip valuations — the crack that matters for services, because the whole Indian AI-transformation pipeline is underwritten by hyperscaler capex plans that assume cheap, abundant compute forever. AI, GenAI & Agents
The frontier is now a treadmill — Claude Opus 5 makes four flagships in two months — Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5, its fourth flagship release in under two months after Mythos 5, Fable 5 and Sonnet 5, alongside OpenAI's GPT-5.6 line and Moonshot's downloadable Kimi K3 weights — a cadence measured in weeks, not quarters, driving inference costs to record lows. The signal for buyers: capability is table stakes and today's frontier is next month's mid-tier, so the winning architecture routes each task to the cheapest tier that clears the bar. Model portability is now a design requirement, not a nice-to-have. Frontier models · Anthropic
The $2/$10 floor expires August 31 — Claude Sonnet 5 — the model that pushed agentic coding into everyday business workflows and has been anchoring the market's price expectations all quarter — currently costs $2 per million input tokens and $10 output. From September 1 it becomes $3/$15, a 50% rise, published in advance. An introductory price with a settlement date isn't a discount, it's a land-grab; anyone whose unit economics currently pencil out at $2/$10 has about six weeks to find out whether the product survives at $3/$15. Pricing · Anthropic

In the same week, LTIMindtree announced a strategic partnership with Anthropic to accelerate enterprise AI adoption, on a +17.1% profit quarter. The two are the same story: every firm racing to deploy agents is widening its own attack surface — so the integrators selling the transformation will increasingly have to sell the agent-security layer alongside it. SaaS, Services & Outsourcing
205,000 gone — and more than half now blame AI — tech layoffs this year total 205,832 workers across 322 events, roughly 1,009 job losses a day. The tell isn't the total, it's the reason: 54% of 2026's layoff events explicitly cite AI, automation or machine learning, affecting 170,945 workers across 173 companies. The biggest cuts of the year: Oracle −30,000 (≈13% of staff), Microsoft −4,800 (mostly Xbox), Monday.com −630 (20% of staff, in an explicit AI pivot). That AI attribution is the line that turns the story from cyclical to structural. Jobs · Global tech
The AI shakeup of a $283bn sector — Reuters frames TCS's layoffs as the leading edge of an AI-driven restructuring of India's $283bn outsourcing industry — the clearest signal yet that the pyramid staffing model, not just this quarter's demand, is what's under pressure. The top-5 majors remain ~46% below their 2024 peak at ₹18.15 lakh crore. When the largest player cuts while reporting a profit beat, it isn't a downturn response; it's a redesign. India IT · Structural
TCS and Infosys tell the same story: flat core, growing AI — TCS opened FY27 with revenue flat at $7,624m (+2.7% YoY) but an annualised AI book of $2.6bn, up 13.6% QoQ and multiple AI-transformation deal wins; Infosys trimmed FY27 guidance to 1.5–3.0% even as its AI revenue reached 8.2% of the mix on a $3.6bn large-deal TCV. Both are buying and re-shaping growth around outcome-priced, AI-delivered work rather than headcount. Deals and margins — not hiring — are what the Street is now paying Indian IT for. India IT · Deals
Job cuts deepen even as the businesses grow — Business Standard reports TCS and Infosys keep laying off while revenue rises, framing it as an AI-first redesign, not distress: TCS has now let go ~23,460 staff (a planned ~12,000-role cut MeitY is watching), while Infosys has sharply slowed fresher onboarding. On the buy side, Infosys completed its acquisition of Optimum Healthcare IT, a Best-in-KLAS digital-transformation firm — buying vertical depth while trimming the pyramid. The pattern holds: shrink the base, bolt on capability. India IT · Restructuring
