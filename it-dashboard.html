The story in one line: the two tech trades stay split — and this week oil is dragging both down together. By Friday, July 24 the anchor had shifted from earnings to energy: Brent broke above $100 a barrel for the first time in two months as the Gulf war ground into a ninth day, feeding a tech-led Wall Street selloff (Dow −0.99% to 51,722) and a fourth straight down session in India (Sensex 76,391, Nifty 23,870). The IT-specific read landed with TCS's Q1: revenue flat at $7,624m (+2.7% YoY) but its annualised AI book hit $2.6bn, up 13.6% QoQ, echoing Infosys's trimmed 1.5–3.0% guidance — flat core, fast AI. The structural divergence is the chart: global investors back the firms building AI while pricing down the services firms whose work it automates. And the capex keeps outrunning the caution — Alphabet lifted 2026 spending to as much as $205bn as Google Cloud grew 82%, while a labs price war (and a fresh Claude Sonnet 5) pins inference at record lows. Underneath it all: the top-5 Indian majors remain ~46% below their 2024 peak, 205,000+ tech jobs are gone in 2026 with 54% of cuts citing AI, and Gartner's $2.59tn AI spend keeps growing 47% against a total IT budget up 10.8%.

Cloud, Infra & Chips
AI servers set to triple — the biggest line item nobody has ordered yet — Gartner expects spending on AI-optimised servers to triple over five years and become the largest single subsegment of AI spending, as cloud providers expand capacity in anticipation of GenAI and agentic workloads. That word — anticipation — is the whole risk. It's a supply-led build-out betting that the agent demand Forrester keeps failing to find in enterprises will eventually show up. Silicon · Capex
The $2.59tn vs $487bn scope trap — Gartner's $2.59 trillion counts the entire AI stack; IDC's $487bn counts AI infrastructure hardware only. They are not competing forecasts of the same thing, and one compiled analysis calls mixing them the single most common analytical error in technology budgeting. Worth knowing before someone puts both on the same slide this quarter — a 5x gap in a headline is almost always a definitional gap, not a disagreement. Analyst · Forecasts
The chip trade wobbles as oil bites — US big-cap tech eased in the latest session (Nasdaq −0.57%, S&P −0.14%) as a rise in crude pressured the broad market. And the structural pressure hasn't gone anywhere: DeepSeek is designing its own inference silicon to escape both Nvidia and Huawei, a frontier lab betting the moat is moving down the stack. It's a reminder that better-and-cheaper compute undercuts the pricing power built into chip valuations — the crack that matters for services, because the whole Indian AI-transformation pipeline is underwritten by hyperscaler capex plans that assume cheap, abundant compute forever. AI, GenAI & Agents
OpenAI turns one model into three price tiers — GPT-5.6 shipped as a lineup, not a flagship: Sol for high-end reasoning, coding and science; Terra at GPT-5.5-class quality for roughly half the cost; and Luna for fast, cheap, high-volume work. It arrived in a July that also saw Google's Gemini 3.6 Flash (Jul 21) and a day when xAI, OpenAI and Meta all launched within 24 hours, driving inference costs to record lows. The signal for buyers: capability is table stakes, and the winning architecture routes each task to the cheapest tier that clears the bar — model portability is now a design requirement, not a nice-to-have. Frontier models · OpenAI
The $2/$10 floor expires August 31 — Claude Sonnet 5 — the model that pushed agentic coding into everyday business workflows and has been anchoring the market's price expectations all quarter — currently costs $2 per million input tokens and $10 output. From September 1 it becomes $3/$15, a 50% rise, published in advance. An introductory price with a settlement date isn't a discount, it's a land-grab; anyone whose unit economics currently pencil out at $2/$10 has about six weeks to find out whether the product survives at $3/$15. Pricing · Anthropic
The first confirmed agent-led breach — and India buys in anyway — Hugging Face disclosed an AI-agent-led intrusion, a rare confirmed case of an autonomous agent used to actually break in rather than merely assist. In the same week, LTIMindtree announced a strategic partnership with Anthropic to accelerate enterprise AI adoption, on a +17.1% profit quarter. The two are the same story: every firm racing to deploy agents is widening its own attack surface — so the integrators selling the transformation will increasingly have to sell the agent-security layer alongside it. Cybersecurity
Nine days of strikes, and now Kyiv is burning too — US forces have bombed Iran for a ninth consecutive day since talks collapsed over a Strait of Hormuz MoU, while Russia hit Kyiv with one of its largest missile barrages of the war, killing at least six. Geopolitics · Continuity
Hormuz: closed, or not, depending on who you ask — Iran declared the strait closed "until further notice" after hitting a third vessel; CENTCOM denied it and began further attacks to guarantee freedom of navigation. On July 7 the LNG tanker Al Rekayat and supertanker Wedyan were struck. A strait is functionally closed the moment underwriters believe it is — so the number that settles this argument is the war-risk premium on a Gulf hull, not a press statement. Geopolitics · Shipping
Anthropic restores Fable 5 globally, with new safeguards — Fable 5 and Mythos 5 came back worldwide on July 1 after US export controls were lifted, shipping with cybersecurity classifiers Anthropic describes as its strongest safeguards to date. It's a small item with a large implication: model availability is now an instrument of trade policy, and enterprises building on a frontier model are, whether they like it or not, exposed to export-control risk. SaaS, Services & Outsourcing
205,000 gone — and more than half now blame AI — tech layoffs this year total 205,832 workers across 322 events, roughly 1,009 job losses a day. The tell isn't the total, it's the reason: 54% of 2026's layoff events explicitly cite AI, automation or machine learning, affecting 170,945 workers across 173 companies. The biggest cuts of the year: Oracle −30,000 (≈13% of staff), Microsoft −4,800 (mostly Xbox), Monday.com −630 (20% of staff, in an explicit AI pivot). That AI attribution is the line that turns the story from cyclical to structural. Jobs · Global tech
The AI shakeup of a $283bn sector — Reuters frames TCS's layoffs as the leading edge of an AI-driven restructuring of India's $283bn outsourcing industry — the clearest signal yet that the pyramid staffing model, not just this quarter's demand, is what's under pressure. The top-5 majors remain ~46% below their 2024 peak at ₹18.15 lakh crore. When the largest player cuts while reporting a profit beat, it isn't a downturn response; it's a redesign. India IT · Structural
TCS and Infosys tell the same story: flat core, growing AI — TCS opened FY27 with revenue flat at $7,624m (+2.7% YoY) but an annualised AI book of $2.6bn, up 13.6% QoQ and multiple AI-transformation deal wins; Infosys trimmed FY27 guidance to 1.5–3.0% even as its AI revenue reached 8.2% of the mix on a $3.6bn large-deal TCV. Both are buying and re-shaping growth around outcome-priced, AI-delivered work rather than headcount. Deals and margins — not hiring — are what the Street is now paying Indian IT for.
